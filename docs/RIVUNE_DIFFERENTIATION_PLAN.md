## Position to earn

Rivune should make a chosen team of AI models useful for everyday answers and finished work. A person chooses an orchestrator and members beside one prompt, then receives a readable result with inspectable contributions and actual checks. Council and Swarm are execution choices within that experience.

Initial audience hypothesis: people already using two supported AI providers who repeatedly compare answers, plan projects or create documents and small websites. A simple experience must eventually include installation and provider onboarding; expecting everyone to know a CLI limits the audience. Validate with users before claiming demand.

## Four priorities

1. **An understandable team.** Per-task orchestrator/member choices, model and reasoning settings, distinct same-CLI entries, and honest availability. Suggest a small team and let users adjust it. Do not show a fabricated intelligence score or account percentage.
2. **Review that improves the result.** Independent drafts, critical assumptions checked against supplied facts, disagreements retained where unresolved, and deterministic checks for explicit machine-checkable requirements. Keep length compliance separate from correctness. A smaller consolidator and extra members are experiments, not defaults justified by intuition.
3. **Finished everyday deliverables.** Treat a decision brief, event plan, document and small website as first-class tasks. Show the finished answer or exact files first, with technical traces collapsed. A worker saying it tested something does not establish that a test ran.
4. **Control over time and work.** Clear selected team, bounded concurrency/repairs, cancellation and retained partial output. Record latency and available usage; unknown usage stays unknown. Automatic routing must stay inside supported capabilities and permissions.

These are places to compete through execution. None is an established exclusive feature or a guarantee of the best answer on every task.

## Experience improvements requested September 6

The user explicitly asks for proactive audits and product ideas, with the ease of chatting in Codex or Claude and inspiration from Traycer. They should not need to discover defects or create more tasks to get the app reviewed. The items below are prioritized acceptance targets for the audit, not claims that features are missing or completed; each reviewer must check current implementation and rendered behavior before assigning a defect.

1. **A calm, familiar workspace.** Keep Accounts at the sidebar bottom, open Settings inside the main window, and provide Graphite, Orbit and Cosmos backgrounds while preserving previous preferences. These are installed in 0619 with bounded rendered checks. Continue checking consistent spacing, readable answer width, keyboard focus and restrained decoration across the rest of the app.
2. **A conversation that survives real work.** Continuing a task should preserve its project, files, chosen team and relevant history. Stop, retry, navigation, Settings and reopening the app must preserve drafts and usable partial output. Show what context is attached and make removing it understandable.
3. **The finished result first.** Present one clear answer or an artifact card with the exact file name, preview and save/open action. Keep member contributions and review details available on demand. An existing file, a proposed code block and a tested artifact must have distinct, truthful states. Continue editing the same deliverable without making users reconstruct the project.
4. **A team people can understand.** Put the orchestrator and team beside the composer, with concise descriptions of Council and Swarm. Display real progress and a clear next action when a member needs input or fails. Use phases and elapsed time instead of fabricated completion percentages. Expose only execution paths that currently work; future Auto/Swarm milestones remain part of the product contract.
5. **A reason to use the team.** Use the prepared matched task evaluation to assess whether review actually improves answers and deliverables over a single member. Retain unresolved disagreements and source uncertainty. Compare usefulness, waiting time and known usage; additional agents alone are not evidence of better quality.

Traycer's current public site documents shared task context, agent-to-agent review and reusable workflows. These are useful patterns to study, not evidence of competitor runtime quality or a reason to copy its branding. Rivune's everyday-task focus remains a positioning hypothesis to validate. ## What would count as better

Use AI_TEAM_EVALUATION_PLAN.md once its independent preparation finishes. Compare the same frozen task with each individual team member and the chosen Council configuration; mask identities for human judging and keep rubrics out of model inputs. Evaluate factual support, completeness, constraints, usefulness and artifact behavior. Record latency, call count and known usage alongside quality, including failures and ties.

For a small pilot, retain the existing proposed target of four of five people finishing a task without intervention and three returning within a week. These are learning targets, not proof of broad superiority.
