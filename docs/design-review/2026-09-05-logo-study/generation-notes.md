# Rivune identity study · September 5, 2026

Four exploratory raster logo concepts generated with the built-in image-generation tool. None replaces the shipping Rivune logo. The accompanying startup timeline is explicitly an animation study; its percentage is simulated for reviewing motion. A real startup percentage must be wired to measurable initialization work without adding artificial delay.

## Shared generation brief

One original Rivune logo per square image, on pure black with no enclosing tile or text. A centered silver/platinum emblem with generous negative space, softly polished face and restrained beveled edges. Prioritize a distinctive silhouette that works small. No other brand marks, watermark, particles or heavy shadow. These are concept renders for later vector refinement.

## Prompt set

1. **Cross** — an X-inspired emblem made of four broad tapered silver arms around diamond-shaped negative space, subtly concave inner edges and clipped outer corners, with an ice-blue center glint. An original four-way junction rather than a plain crossed pair of strokes.
2. **Converge** — refine the supplied current Rivune app icon: preserve its two upper wings converging into a short split stem, simplify the notches, keep a pearl/ice-blue center diamond, remove the textured rounded-square background. Reference: `brand-assets/RivuneAppIcon-master.png`.
3. **Ribbon** — an abstract R monogram from one folded platinum ribbon: compact open upper loop, short left upright, diagonal lower-right leg, and a dark fold break. No infinity or chain-link silhouette.
4. **Halo** — two opposing substantial curved platinum arcs, intentional diagonal openings, and a small pearl/ice-blue center dot. A near-circular orbit expressing two perspectives around one answer.

## Naming and prototype scope

The optional interior concept explores “ChatGPT” as a display label and explicitly identifies its current connection as “via Codex CLI.” The actual runtime invokes Codex CLI; this concept does not add a direct ChatGPT integration, API execution, authentication or live connectivity. No production app labels or runtime code are changed by the study.
