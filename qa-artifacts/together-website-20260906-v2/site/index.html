This month we are reading

The Cartographer of Rain

A novel by Mira Vale

A mapmaker arrives in a drought-struck harbor town carrying charts of storms that have not happened yet. Quietly uncanny and rich with moral tension, Vale’s novel asks what we owe one another when certainty becomes a kind of shelter.

“A book about prediction that keeps returning us to the untidy courage of paying attention.”

Come as you are

An evening at Lantern Pages

You do not need a polished interpretation or a stack of notes. Curiosity is enough. We keep the group conversational, make room for quieter voices, and leave with more questions than we brought.

  1. 7:00

    Settle in

    Tea is poured, chairs are gathered, and introductions happen without an icebreaker.

  2. 7:20

    Open the book

    One reader begins with a passage, then the room follows what feels alive—character, craft, disagreement, or surprise.

  3. 8:35

    Turn the page

    We close with lingering thoughts and reveal the next selection. Conversation often continues on the walk home.

A small courtesy

Spoilers are welcome; certainty is optional. We discuss ideas generously and never confuse a strong opinion with the final word.

Before you arrive

Useful questions

Do I need to finish the book?

No. For the October 17 gathering, read as much of pages 1–168 as you can. The conversation may include details from that portion, but unfinished readers are warmly welcome.

Can I come on my own?

Absolutely. Most regulars first arrived alone, and the opening few minutes are set aside for simple introductions.

Is the venue accessible?

The Juniper Room at 42 Alder Lane is step-free, with an accessible restroom and armless seating. Seating can be rearranged as needed.

Does it cost anything?

No. Lantern Pages is free to attend, no reservation is required, and there is never an expectation to buy the featured book.
