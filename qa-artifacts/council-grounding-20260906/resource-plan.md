## How many can complete: 18

Kits are the binding constraint. Seats allow 24 people at once (2 × 12), the 90-minute booking allows two consecutive 45-minute sessions, and two volunteers can staff two rooms simultaneously — but only 18 kits exist, each is consumed entirely by one person, and none can be obtained before the booking. So **18 is the maximum number who can complete the hands-on activity, and 18 will complete it provided at least 18 of the selected participants and standbys are present.** No scheduling arrangement raises that number; running a second round would only redistribute the same 18 kits across more clock time.

## Plan

**Room split: 9 hands-on + 3 non-participants per room, one volunteer each.** This fills both rooms to exactly their 12-seat capacity and keeps a 9:1 ratio in each. A 12/6 split also fits the seat and volunteer limits, so it is not infeasible — it just concentrates all six non-participants in one room and gives one volunteer twice the load of the other. I recommend 9/9.

**Single parallel round, not two sequential rounds.** Both rooms run the 45-minute session at the same time, each with one volunteer. Two rounds would consume the entire 90 minutes, leaving no margin for check-in or teardown, and would produce no additional completions.

**Schedule (T = start of the booked 90 minutes):**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| T+00 – T+10 | Check-in; confirm the 18 allocations; reassign kits from no-shows in standby order |
| T+10 – T+15 | Seating; distribute kits **still sealed** |
| T+15 – T+60 | Hands-on session, 45 minutes, one volunteer per room |
| T+60 – T+75 | Debrief and Q&A, both rooms |
| T+75 – T+90 | Kit disposal, room reset |

The session must begin no later than T+30 or it will not finish inside the booking. If check-in overruns, shorten the briefing rather than the session.

**Two operational rules that protect the count of 18:**
- Do not unseal a kit until its assigned person is seated. An opened kit for a no-show is a permanently lost completion.
- Do not spend a kit on a facilitator dry run. That would cut completions to 17. Rehearse with an empty or mock kit.

## Handling the 6 who do not get a kit

**Announce the shortage before the event, not at the door.** Tell all 24 registrants that 18 kits exist and state the selection method in advance.

**Ask for voluntary deferrals first.** Offer a guaranteed, pre-assigned slot at a future session with no re-registration. Some registrants have scheduling flexibility, and this may resolve part or all of the gap without a draw.

**If more than 18 still want a kit, use a documented random draw**, announced beforehand and run visibly (or with a published seed). Random is preferable to first-come-first-served or first-to-arrive: registration order was never communicated as a rationing rule, and rewarding arrival time penalizes people with commutes or care obligations. Record the draw order as a standby list; if a selected participant does not arrive by a stated cutoff, their sealed kit goes to the first standby present.

**Be explicit that observing is not completing.** The six non-participants have not performed the activity. Attendance records, certificates, and any reported completion count must say 18, not 24. What they can be offered instead, described honestly as different rather than equivalent:
- A seat in the room with a defined role — timing, note-taking, capturing the group write-up, questions in the debrief — which is participation in the workshop, not completion of the activity.
- First claim on any kit freed by a no-show at this session.
- First priority, pre-assigned slots at the next session, confirmed in writing before they leave, ahead of new registrations.
- Leaving instead of observing should carry no penalty to that priority.

**Conditional:** if registrants paid specifically for hands-on completion, a refund or transfer is the appropriate remedy rather than an observer role. The task states there is no money to obtain kits; it does not say whether registration was paid, so treat this as an if-then, not an established fact.

## Uncertainty and what was not verified

This is a paper plan. **No tests, simulations, or dry runs were performed**, and nothing here rests on tool execution. The schedule above is a proposed timeline, not a validated one.

Assumptions that would change the schedule if wrong:
- The 90 minutes is room-access time, with setup and teardown inside it. Earlier access makes the timeline comfortable rather than tight.
- The 45-minute session length includes in-session cleanup.
- Kits require no preparation or activation lead time; if they do, that time comes out of the 30-minute pre-session window.
- "One volunteer present" means one per concurrent room, so both volunteers are committed during the session and neither can staff a separate activity.

Worth confirming before the day: expected no-show rate among the 24, and whether any registrant has an accessibility or scheduling need that should be honored ahead of the random draw. If fewer than 18 attend, everyone present who wants a kit gets one and the draw is moot.
