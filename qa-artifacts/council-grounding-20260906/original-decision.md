## Recommendation

Run the **weekly video group** for all six weeks. Defer the monthly in-person group and the quarterly public event.

## Rationale

Three supplied facts decide this:

- **20 interested readers, 12 seats.** Only the video format can accommodate everyone. Any in-person start excludes at least 8 of the 20 by construction, before you know who actually turns up.
- **Six weeks is a short window.** Weekly gives you roughly five sessions of signal. Monthly gives you one, possibly two. Quarterly gives you at most one event and probably zero, since the window is under half a quarter — that is all preparation and no learning.
- **The binding constraint is 8 combined volunteer hours per week, not money.** A recurring video session has no venue coordination, setup, or teardown. A public event concentrates the largest share of scarce hours into the single format that produces the least repeat data.

## Schedule

Four volunteers, 8 combined hours per week (~2 hours each).

| Week | Activity | Est. combined hours |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm video tool and reading material at zero cost; assign roles; invite all 20; set the recurring time | ~5 |
| 2 | Session 1; follow up with non-attendees | ~4 |
| 3 | Session 2 | ~4 |
| 4 | Session 3; mid-pilot review of attendance, return rate, and hours logged | ~5 |
| 5 | Session 4; adjust facilitation or session time within the same format | ~4 |
| 6 | Session 5; short participant survey; decide the next format | ~5 |

Roles rotate across the four volunteers: facilitator, co-facilitator/tech support, communications, and attendance/feedback tracking. The rotation matters because it keeps any session from depending on one person.

The hour figures are **estimates derived from assumed session length, not supplied facts**. If week 1 setup runs over, hold the first session in week 2 rather than borrowing hours from the ceiling.

## What to measure

These are **proposed** measures. Nothing here has been run or tested; there are no results to report.

1. Attendance per session, and unique readers reached across all five, against the baseline of 20.
2. Return rate — how many attend a second time, and how many attend three or more sessions.
3. Readers who could not participate, with the stated reason (technology access vs. scheduling). Separating these two matters, because they point to different fixes.
4. Combined volunteer hours logged per week against the 8-hour ceiling, and how they distribute across the four volunteers.
5. Whether discussion stays workable at the attendance level observed.
6. Brief end-of-pilot feedback: would you return, and do you prefer video or in person?

## Conditions that would change the recommendation

- **Switch to the monthly in-person group** if, after three sessions, fewer than half of the 20 have attended at least once *and* the stated reason is video access rather than interest. This fallback caps reach at 12 and needs an explicit waitlist or rotation policy, so it trades reach for accessibility rather than strictly improving on the video group.
- **Also consider in-person** if attendance settles at or below 12 regulars who say they prefer meeting in person — at that point the seat limit stops binding and the format choice is genuinely open.
- **Reduce cadence before changing format** if logged hours exceed 8 in two consecutive weeks. Drop to biweekly video first; cadence is the only lever that costs nothing.
- **Revisit the quarterly public event** only if demand exceeds 20 with a waitlist while hours stay under budget. That will not resolve inside six weeks.

## Assumptions (not supplied in the brief)

- **A1.** A free video service exists that can host 20 participants. Free tiers often impose participant or duration limits. **Unverified — check in week 1.**
- **A2.** Readers have devices and connectivity. **Unverified and material**; if this fails, the whole recommendation fails.
- **A3.** Reading material is obtainable at no cost to the club or its readers. No materials budget was stated.
- **A4.** Sessions run about one hour, and the hour estimates above follow from that.
- **A5.** "Interested" does not equal "attending"; expect fewer than 20 at any session.
- **A6.** The 8 hours is a firm weekly ceiling, and volunteers can meet at a time readers can attend. Nothing was supplied about when anyone is available.
- **A7.** The "half after three sessions" threshold is a suggested decision rule, not a prediction.

Run the A1–A3 checks before announcing a schedule to the 20 readers. If any fails, go straight to the monthly in-person fallback with a 12-person cap.
