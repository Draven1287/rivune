## Note to the library manager

**To:** Library Manager
**Re:** Reminder messages A and B

**What we saw:** Group A, 9 of 12 returned within a week (75%). Group B, 7 of 12 (58%) — a gap of two readers, about 17 percentage points.

**What it supports:** only a description of these 24 readers. Two readers is a small margin in groups this size; if two more Group B readers had returned within the week, the rates would be equal. The result is consistent with A being better, B being better, or no real difference.

**Should we claim A caused better returns?** No. Readers were not randomly assigned and the groups came from different neighborhoods, so a neighborhood difference could produce this gap on its own. With no earlier return rates, we cannot check whether the groups were similar beforehand. We can say A's rate was higher here; we cannot say the message is why.

**Suggested next test** (a proposal, not yet run): within each neighborhood, randomly assign borrowers to message A or B over the same period, so both messages face similar conditions. Fix the outcome in advance as "returned within seven days," analyze readers by assigned message, and enroll far more than twelve per group — twelve cannot resolve a difference this size.

**Assumption (unverified):** the library can send either message and log each borrower's assigned message and return date consistently.

*(Note is 225 words.)*

### Review note

Both drafts agreed on the core: the data are descriptive only, causal attribution is blocked by non-random assignment plus neighborhood confounding and the absence of baseline rates, and the next step is a randomized comparison. Neither ran any test or tool, and neither is claimed to have; the proposed trial is a suggestion only.

Contributions used:
- **Draft 1** — the framing (observation / what it supports / causal claim / next test), the "two readers is within ordinary variation" point, pre-specifying the seven-day outcome, and the explicit reminder that twelve per arm is too few.
- **Draft 2** — the percentage-point framing, analyzing readers by *assigned* message (intention-to-treat), and randomizing *within* neighborhoods.

Corrections made:
- Draft 1's "if two Group B readers had returned a day earlier" assumes facts about when non-returners returned; nothing supplied says that. Rephrased to the supported counterfactual: two more returns within the week would equalize the rates.
- Draft 1's "several hundred borrowers" implies a power calculation that was not supplied; replaced with "far more than twelve per group."
- Drafts conflicted on neighborhoods: Draft 1 would ignore them so they split across arms; Draft 2 would randomize within them. I adopted Draft 2's blocking, which guarantees rather than merely expects balance on the one confounder we know about.

Arithmetic checked: 9/12 = 75%, 7/12 = 58.3%, gap 16.7 pp ("about 17"). Unresolved and left visible: whether these neighborhoods remain available for a future trial, and whether readers overlap between groups, are not stated in the supplied facts.
