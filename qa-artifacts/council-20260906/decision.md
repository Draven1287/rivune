## Recommendation

**Run a weekly video group for four sessions (weeks 2–5), then hold one in-person meeting in week 6 in place of that week's video session.** Treat the quarterly public event as out of scope for this window.

### Rationale (from the supplied facts only)

- **A quarterly event produces no evidence in six weeks.** The first event would fall around week 12, so all six weeks would be prep with zero reader contact.
- **Monthly in-person yields one meeting, maybe two.** That is too few points to see whether people come back — which is the question that decides whether the club exists.
- **Weekly video fits the numbers.** All 20 interested readers can attend; the 12-seat venue cannot hold them, so in-person as the *only* format means turning people away at first contact.
- **The hours fit.** 8 combined hours ÷ 4 volunteers = 2 hours each per week. Estimated weekly load is roughly 4.5–5 hours (breakdown below), leaving 3–3.5 hours of slack.
- **The week-6 in-person meeting converts "monthly in-person" from a deferred option into a tested one.** Putting it *instead of*, not alongside, video session 5 is deliberate: asking readers for two meetings in one week would depress in-person turnout and confound the comparison you are trying to make. The cost is one fewer video data point (4 instead of 5); that is worth a clean format comparison at the decision point.

### Assumptions (not supplied facts — verify these in week 1)

1. A no-cost video tool is available. Free tiers may impose a per-session time limit; confirm the limit before committing to 60-minute sessions.
2. Readers have a device and workable internet.
3. Reading material must be free (public domain or library-available), and weekly cadence requires **short** readings — a book per week will not survive.
4. The venue is available to us at no cost. Nothing in the supplied facts says this; if it isn't free, week 6 changes.
5. "Interested" ≠ committed. Plan for roughly half of the 20 at session 1.
6. The 8 hours are usable in small asynchronous blocks, not one shared meeting.
7. Sessions run 60 minutes.

## Six-week schedule

| Week | Volunteer work | Reader-facing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm tool and its time limit; pick first 4 readings; assign roles; free signup sheet; invite with two candidate time slots | Invite all 20; time poll closes |
| 2 | Confirm slot; send reading 1 + link | **Video session 1** |
| 3 | Reminder + reading 2 | **Video session 2** |
| 4 | Reminder + reading 3; 30-min volunteer check-in | **Video session 3** |
| 5 | Reminder + reading 4; open in-person RSVP, cap 12, waitlist beyond | **Video session 4** |
| 6 | Venue setup; one-question preference poll after | **In-person meeting, capped at 12** |

**Roles, one per volunteer, ~1–1.5 h/week each:** Host (runs the session), Prep (picks reading, writes 3–4 questions), Comms (invites, reminders, replies), Logistics/Data (signup sheet, attendance log, venue).

**Weekly estimate:** host + co-host in session 2.0 h; prep 1.0 h; comms 1.0 h; data/admin 0.5 h ≈ **4.5 h of 8**. Week 6 runs higher (setup and travel); the slack absorbs it. These estimates are judgment, not measurements — log actuals.

## What to measure

All of these are free to collect on one sheet.

1. **Interest → attendance:** of 20 invited, how many attend session 1.
2. **Core group size:** people attending ≥3 of the 4 video sessions. This is the club's real size and the most important number here.
3. **Attendance trend** across sessions 1–4. A steady decline by session 3 means the format or the reading is wrong.
4. **Read rate:** how many arrived having done the reading. Low read rate → shorten the reading, not the discussion.
5. **Volunteer hours actually spent per week**, against the 8-hour ceiling. This is your burnout warning.
6. **Access failures and reasons for absence.**
7. **In-person RSVPs vs. show-ups, and whether RSVPs exceeded 12.** This directly tests whether the seat cap binds.
8. **One-question preference poll** in week 6: in-person / video / either.

## Conditions that would change the recommendation

- **Switch to monthly in-person (RSVP cap 12 + waitlist)** if week-6 RSVPs meet or exceed the 12 seats while video attendance has fallen to roughly 4 or fewer.
- **Drop cadence to biweekly or monthly immediately** if logged volunteer hours exceed 8/week for two consecutive weeks. The hour ceiling is a supplied fact; the cadence is a choice.
- **Abandon the video pilot early** if fewer than ~6 readers attend two consecutive sessions after reminders, or if access problems repeatedly block participation. Test monthly in-person instead.
- **Start in-person from week 2** if assumption 1 or 2 fails outright. The seat cap then forces RSVP-and-waitlist from day one, and you accept turning readers away.
- **Prioritize the quarterly public event** only if the real goal is visibility or recruitment rather than a reading habit, or if an external date is fixed. It is a marketing instrument, not a club.

**Note on the ~6-attendee floor:** this is a proposed decision threshold, not something derivable from the supplied facts. Set it before session 1 so it isn't rationalized afterward.

## Uncertainty

No sessions were run, no messages sent, no tools or tests executed, and no external sources consulted. Every number here except the arithmetic — 8 h ÷ 4 volunteers = 2 h each, and 20 readers against 12 seats — is an estimate to be replaced by measurement. The schedule is designed so that week 6 supplies real figures.
